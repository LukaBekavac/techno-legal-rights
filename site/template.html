Data for “Techno-Legal Rights and Exercise Friction in EU Digital Law” · legal texts as of 9 Oct 2026

Rights you can exercise under EU digital law

Every right a person or an organisation can invoke under 112 texts of EU digital law, grouped by their status in a 2023 overview of EU digital legislation. Each quote appears word for word in the act. The rights were extracted and classified with a large language model. Only the 497 rights in the twelve acts analysed in the paper were checked by the author: choose “Paper sample” under Techno-legal rights to see them.

Techno-legal rights

The paper’s definition and test, applied to every right by the model: a mechanism the duty-bearer must provide or permit, and technical ability on the part of the holder. Dots show the level on the gradient, as in Figure 1 of the paper.

Paper and data

Bekavac, Techno-Legal Rights and Exercise Friction in EU Digital Law (under submission). Data, legal texts, scripts and the instructions given to the model: github.com/LukaBekavac/techno-legal-rights.

Source

The legal texts behind Table 1 of J. Scott Marcus, Kamil Sekut and Kai Zenner, A Dataset on EU Legislation for the Digital World (Bruegel, 2023), with their status updated to 9 October 2026. The status groups follow the table’s cell colours. Texts were downloaded from the EU Publications Office.

Which text was read

Laws in force: the latest consolidated version. Files in negotiation in 2023: the adopted act where there is one, otherwise the Commission proposal. Withdrawn proposals are included and marked.

Limits

Extracted and classified by a large language model (Claude Opus 5.5) following written rules; every quote and article number was checked automatically. Only the twelve acts of the paper were checked by the author. Not legal advice. Rights in directives reach people through national law. Proposal texts can still change. Annexes were mostly not read.
